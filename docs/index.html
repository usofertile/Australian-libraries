Checked Out?

How Australia's public libraries are changing, and who they serve.

By Dylan Lim Gen Yuan · October 2026

Ask most Australians when they last borrowed a library book and many will say “at school”. Yet in 2024–25, people in New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland walked into their public libraries 69 million times. Libraries haven't checked out. They've changed.

  • 69 million library visits in a year: 3.3 for every resident NSW, Victoria and Queensland, 2024–25
  • 1 in 3 residents is a library member 6.9 million members, 2024–25
  • 1 in 4 loans in Victoria and Queensland is now digital e-books, e-audiobooks and e-video, 2024–25

01

Who walks through the door?

One in five adults and one in three children visited a library in 2021–22. Who goes depends on your age, your gender and where you live.

Every few years the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) asks Australians which cultural venues they have visited. Children are the library's most loyal users: in every state and territory, they are far more likely than adults to have been.

How to read this map

Each square is a state or territory, drawn the same size so that the ACT and Tasmania are as easy to compare as Western Australia. Darker squares mean a higher share of people visited a library.

Library use follows the rhythm of life. It peaks in the storytime years, falls away through the teens and twenties, and rises again when people have children of their own and after they retire. At every age, women are more likely than men to visit.

Age and gender shape who visits. Income, surprisingly, does not.

02

Libraries are for everyone

Most cultural outings cost money, and it shows. Libraries are the exception.

Australians in the highest income group are almost three times as likely as those in the lowest to visit an art gallery or a museum, and four times as likely to see a musical. At the library the share barely moves: about one in five, whatever people earn. Borrowing from a public library is free, and it shows in who walks in.

The one gap is education: 31% of people with a postgraduate degree visit a library, compared with 16% of those who left school at Year 10.

If libraries are for everyone, can everyone reach one?

03

Libraries reach every corner

From city branches to outback towns, public libraries stretch across the continent, but how much people borrow varies hugely from place to place.

Western Australia shows the scale of the network. Three in ten of its public libraries are within 50 km of Perth, yet the network stretches more than 2,000 km north to Kununurra. Drag across the distance chart to see where each group of libraries sits.

In NSW, borrowing per person varies more than ten-fold between councils. Lane Cove, on Sydney's lower north shore, lends almost 16 items per resident a year; nearby Fairfield lends fewer than two. Coastal regional councils such as Byron and Ballina rival the busiest city libraries.

So what are people borrowing?

04

Print holds steady, digital grows faster

E-books are the fastest-growing part of library lending, but they are adding to print, not replacing it.

Picture books and junior fiction alone make up one in three loans in NSW. Together, children's and teen books are borrowed more often than books for adults.

Victoria's three years of data show the shift. E-book loans grew by 36% in two years, five times faster than loans of physical items, while libraries trimmed their physical collections. Inside the building, most internet sessions are now on visitors' own phones and laptops, over the library's Wi-Fi.

Fewer people need a library computer. More need a place to learn and meet.

05

More than books

Storytime, coding clubs, book groups and job help: libraries in NSW and Queensland ran more than 175,000 program sessions in 2024–25, and the youngest Australians are their biggest audience.

Early years, every week

15,700 Victorian children

attended an early-years session at their library each week in 2024–25, along with about 12,500 parents and carers. Storytime and rhyme time build the language and reading skills children take to school.

Source: Public Libraries Victoria, 2024–25.

Libraries are also becoming places to meet. In Victoria, hours booked in library meeting rooms rose by almost three-quarters in two years, local partnerships grew by 52% and book clubs by 43%: all far faster than the population.

In the bush, the library matters even more. Queensland's small rural councils, supported by Rural Libraries Queensland, record more visits and twice as many program attendances per resident as councils that run their own library service, even though their residents borrow fewer than half as many digital items.

About the data

  • ABS figures are from 2021–22, the latest release. NSW and Victoria had long COVID lockdowns in 2021.
  • The ABS counts libraries and archives together, and children's visits outside school hours only.
  • Victorian figures exclude Vision Australia so that the three years compare like with like.
  • NSW and Queensland classify library programs differently.
  • Per-resident figures for very small councils can be inflated by visitors or shared libraries.
